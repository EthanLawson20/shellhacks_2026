The problem

After a collapse, the hardest call is where to dig first.

Sound can mislead

At the 2021 Surfside collapse, crews had to chase every noise without knowing whether it was a person tapping or debris settling.

Silence hides people

Someone who is unconscious but breathing makes no sound for a listening device to hear.

Radar doesn’t scale

Tools that detect breathing cost thousands of dollars each and scan one spot at a time.

The approach

Cheap nodes.
Two kinds of evidence.
One ranked list.

  1. 1

    Place the mesh

    One sender node broadcasts Wi-Fi packets nonstop. Three or more receiver nodes sit around the debris pile or building.

    1 sender, 3+ receivers
  2. 2

    Sense and listen

    Each receiver measures how the Wi-Fi signal shifts, called channel state information (CSI), to catch motion and breathing. Its microphone labels sound as human or not, right on the device.

    Wi-Fi CSI + on-device audio
  3. 3

    Rank the zones

    The dashboard combines both signals for each zone and keeps a priority list sorted as new readings arrive.

    Updates with every reading

The priority

Sound and Wi-Fi
check each other.

The microphone hears voices and tapping. The Wi-Fi mesh picks up motion and breathing. Scored together, they separate a survivor who can call for help from one who can’t.
